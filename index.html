Abstract

A better place to synthesize.

The paper couples a hybrid re-sampling pipeline with a learned interpolation factor. The goal is not more synthetic data; it is synthetic data that lands on the right side of the boundary.

Anomaly detection of gas turbines faces the significant challenges of data imbalance and inter-class overlap. In this paper, we develop a novel data augmentation method, namely deep attention synthetic minority over-sampling technique with the Encoder-Decoder (DA-SMOTE-ED), which serves as a key step in our hybrid re-sampling scheme.

To reduce the risk of generating noise data, on one hand, the DA-SMOTE-ED leverages an Encoder-Decoder to learn a class-separable feature space to weaken the effect of inter-class overlap. On the other hand, an attention module is applied to assign proper interpolation factors to generate synthetic samples that stay off the aggregation area of normal samples. Synthetic samples are generated in the learnable feature space, mapped back to the original space, and merged with under-sampled samples to form the balanced dataset.

The method is validated on real gas-turbine monitoring data and the modified C-MAPPS dataset. Its average balanced accuracy is 91.77% on the gas turbine dataset, yielding 3.67%, 6.4%, and 5.56% improvements compared to SMOTE-ENN, TimeGAN, and AugmentTS, respectively.

01

Compress, don't discard

K-Means keeps K normal cluster centers instead of throwing the majority class away. The under-sampled set is K centers plus every abnormal sample.

02

Separate before synthesizing

Two Transformer encoder layers map the data to a latent space. A triplet-center objective pulls each class inward and pushes the other class away.

03

Learn one α per pair

SMOTE samples α from U(0, 1). DA-SMOTE runs a small attention block over each sample pair and predicts the factor from the data distribution.

04

Test both regimes

The same recipe is checked on a real fleet with a 15:1 imbalance and on a public turbofan benchmark with a 66:1 imbalance.

The failure mode

A random step can land in the wrong neighbourhood.

Standard SMOTE interpolates toward a minority neighbour with no look at the majority distribution. When the two classes overlap, a synthetic fault can fall inside normal operation and teach the classifier the wrong boundary.

Method

One pipeline. Four moves.

From raw flight-cycle windows to a balanced training set and a binary decision. The encoder is not an add-on: it is what makes the interpolation factor worth learning.

01

Compress the majority

K-Means partitions normal windows into K clusters. The K centers replace the full normal set, then join every abnormal window to form the under-sampled set X.

K = 90K centers + M minorities
02

Learn a separable space

A two-layer Transformer encoder maps X to Z. The reconstruction loss keeps the input information; the triplet-center loss pulls each class toward its center and pushes the other class away.

2 encoder layersh = 4d_i = 32
03

Predict alpha per pair

For each abnormal sample z and one of its 5 nearest abnormal neighbours z̃, the attention block predicts an interpolation factor in (0, 1). The synthetic point is z + α̂(z̃ − z).

k = 5reduction r = 16sigmoid output
04

Decode and classify

The decoder maps synthetic latent samples back to the original space. Under-sampled and synthetic samples train the SANet classifier, which returns a normal or abnormal decision.

2 decoder layersSANet + Softmax

Held constant

The classifier is the same for every method.

All 14 methods are evaluated with the same SANet architecture and hyper-parameters. That isolates the effect of re-sampling: changes in TPR, TNR, and balanced accuracy are not coming from a larger or differently tuned classifier.

14 methods, one test protocol. Baseline, 2 under-samplers, 4 over-samplers, 2 hybrid methods, and 4 deep generative methods.
5-fold cross-validation on both datasets, reporting TPR, TNR, and balanced accuracy.
Inference cost is essentially flat across methods because every experiment uses the trained SANet at test time.
Inside DA-SMOTE

The factor is not sampled. It is predicted.

A small attention block reads the pair [z, z̃] and returns a scalar. The rest is still interpolation — but the step size now depends on where the pair sits in the learned space.

FIG. 04 / DA-SMOTE-EDENLARGE

Fig. 4. (a) Encoder → DA-SMOTE → decoder. (b) The attention block: linear → ReLU → linear → ReLU → linear → sigmoid, producing α̂.

Attention factorEquations 5–8
h1 = ReLU(W1[z, z̃])
h2 = ReLU(W2h1)
α̂ = σ(W3h2) ∈ (0, 1)
znew = z + α̂(z̃ − z)

The first linear layer reduces dimensionality with ratio r = 16; the second restores it. The sigmoid keeps the interpolation factor inside the segment between the two abnormal samples.

Reconstruction
LR — keep the input

Class-balanced l2 reconstruction, with β = 0.999. Prevents the latent space from throwing away the signal.

Triplet-center
LTC — separate the classes

Margin d = 5. Pulls each sample toward its class center and pushes it away from the other center.

End-to-end objective
LTotal = LR + LTC

The encoder, attention block and decoder are trained together, so the interpolation factor is shaped by both reconstruction fidelity and class separation.

Read TPR and TNR together.

The reported TNR is lower than the baselines because the classifier is not dominated by the majority class. The authors argue this is the appropriate operating point for safety-critical gas turbines, where missing a fault is more costly than a false alarm. Balanced accuracy rises because the TPR gain is larger than the TNR loss.

The geometry moves.

The method is not only a table result. The paper shows the learned distribution shifting during training, and the t-SNE geometry before and after re-sampling.

training · pull & push

A synthetic point is trained, not painted.

The triplet-center objective gives the synthetic sample its own loss term. As training proceeds, it is pulled toward the abnormal center and pushed away from the normal center — the geometric argument behind “stay off the danger area.”

Separability survives re-sampling.

Under-sampling alone thins the majority class but does not create clean separation. The encoder changes the geometry: after projection, the classes form distinct groups. Adding DA-SMOTE-ED samples does not collapse them back together.

Scope, not hype

  • Binary anomaly detection. The multiclass extension is presented as a derivation, not as an experimentally validated study.
  • One real fleet. The turbine data come from an Asian airline; fault onsets are not precisely known and labels are tied to maintenance reports.
  • Lower TNR. The method trades normal-class recall for abnormal-class recall; balanced accuracy is the headline metric, not TNR alone.
  • No released code. The paper reports hyper-parameters and data sources but no public implementation link.
